Why it exists

          
Raids move faster than one moderator

          
In 2021, waves of bot-driven hate raids hit Twitch streamers hard enough that many of them organised #ADayOffTwitch. StreamCrew is built for that moment: one grouped incident, one human decision, one record of who made it.

        

        
      

    


    
    

      

        

          
Architecture

          
Why Switch Is Essential: Separation of Powers

          
A terminal engine works for one developer. Switch turns StreamCrew into a multi-person incident gate with role boundaries.

        

        

          

            01 · ROLE BOUNDARIES
            
Two Humans & Two Specialized Agents

            
The streamer stays focused on the broadcast while the moderator triages in #stream-ops. @crew-mod handles incident queues; @crew-producer drafts and validates JSON rules.

            Participants: Streamer, Mod, @crew-mod, @crew-producer
            

              
StreamerON AIR

              
Sam · moderatorAPPROVES

              
@crew-modQUEUE

              
@crew-producerRULES

            

          

          

            02 · ZERO TOKEN EXPOSURE
            
Agents Never Hold Twitch OAuth

            
Twitch credentials stay isolated inside the local engine’s .env. Switch agents only invoke safe crew CLI subcommands and receive redacted chat samples.

            redact.py strips URLs & emails before room display
            
bot_3: FREE xxxxxxx at [link removed]
bot_4: FREE xxxxxxx at [link removed]

          

          

            03 · LOCKED BATCH & AUDIT TRAIL
            
Target Count Guard & --by Attribution

            
Viewing an approval locks its target list (locked: true) so continuing raids cannot silently expand a batch before approval. Every decision logs the human moderator’s name.

            crew approve 1 --count 6 --by Sam
            
$ crew approve 1 --count 6 --by Sam
approval 1: done | by=Sam

Workflows

          
Verified Capabilities & Honest Engineering Limits

          
Built on deterministic detectors, mocked transport tests, and transparent operational boundaries.

        

        

          

            

              

              

                

                  
Detects 5+ distinct accounts posting normalized identical text within 10 seconds (capped at 25 targets per batch). Executes sequential 0.5s paced Helix timeouts on human approval.

                  1,000 messages scanned in 10.8 ms (<100 ms budget)
                

              

            

            

              

              

                

                  
Ask @crew-producer for a raid rule (20+ viewers → shoutout + stream marker). It writes to rules/draft/, runs crew validate and crew test, and waits for “ship it”.

                  Dynamic rule reload without restarting crew listen
                

              

            

            

              

              

                

                  
Demo bursts use crew simulate spam through the real detector. Never approve simulated fixture IDs with DRY_RUN=false (use simulate test-alt for owned alt accounts).

                  No slow-mode / subscribe / cheer scopes required
